# CHAPTER 8: THE OTHER

There is a strange moment when you realize that someone is watching you. You may have been lost inside your own world, but suddenly you look up and someone is looking at you. 

Nothing physical has changed, yet the world feels different. You are no longer simply seeing; you are being seen.

## The Gaze
Something in the I changes when another I enters its awareness. You become conscious of your posture, your movement, your voice. You wonder what is happening inside the mind of the person looking at you. 
I cannot see it directly. I can see their eyes and hear their words, but I cannot enter the experience behind those eyes. I cannot step outside myself and temporarily become them.

## The discovery of other worlds
Perhaps this is one of the most important discoveries the I makes: **I am not alone in being an I.**
The world contains other centers of experience. Other bodies, other memories, other fears. 
When I was young, I knew my mother through her functions: she was food, warmth, and safety. But eventually, the functions become a person. Mother becomes someone who had a childhood before I existed, someone with dreams and pains I did not know. I encounter another I.

## Theory of Mind and Inference
I know my own pain from the inside. I know what a thought feels like while I am thinking it. But I do not know your experience that way. I know only my experience of *you*.
From fragments of your expressions and actions, I construct an idea of who you are. We are two private worlds trying to establish contact.

## The mirror that looks back
Unlike the physical mirror, this social mirror interprets me. I am a subject to myself, but to you, I initially appear as an object. Somewhere between these two representations, we attempt to meet.
Contact is never perfect. We can love, argue, and cooperate, but we can also be completely convinced and completely wrong about each other.

## The Influence
The I that once seemed solitary now appears to have been shaped by others from the beginning. My language, my name, and my beliefs were introduced by others. Even the story I tell about myself contains voices that did not originate within me.

If other people help construct the I, where does my I end and the influence of another begin?
When I make a choice that feels completely mine—how much of that choice came from me?
The I has discovered the Other. Now it must discover how deeply the Other lives inside it.
