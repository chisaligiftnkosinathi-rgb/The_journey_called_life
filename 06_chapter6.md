# CHAPTER 6: THE MIRROR

There is a strange moment in every human life that almost all of us have forgotten. We look into a mirror. At first, there is only a face—two eyes, a nose, a mouth. Then, somehow, the face becomes *mine*.

No one remembers the first time they understood that the person in the glass was not another child standing on the other side. Yet somewhere in becoming human, the reflection stopped being merely an image and became a self.

## The threshold
Before the mirror, I experience the world. In the mirror, I encounter myself as something that can be seen. The eye that looks outward is suddenly looking at the one who owns the eye.
The mirror gives us an image of the person. But somehow, the person is looking at the image.
Who is the I? And who is the self being looked at?

## The second mirror: Other people
A physical mirror shows me my face. Other people become another kind of mirror.
Someone calls my name. Someone praises me; someone fears me.
Gradually, I discover something profound: **I am not only someone who experiences the world. I am also someone who exists in other people’s worlds.**
This realization changes the structure of the I. I begin to wonder: *How do they see me?* and eventually, *Who am I when nobody is looking?*

## The handle: The Name
At first, a name like "Gift" is just a sound. Eventually, the sound becomes associated with this body, these actions, and these memories.
The name becomes a handle attached to the self-model. It is a symbol pointing toward the I. But if you removed the name, the I would not disappear.
We begin to separate the layers: the body, the name, the face, the memory, the social identity, and the observer. They feel like one thing, but they are an assembly.

## The biological anchor: Proprioception
Long before the mirror, the body provides information about itself. Close your eyes and raise your hand; you know where it is without looking.
This is "proprioception"—the pre-reflective bodily I.
The mirror does not necessarily create the self; it reveals another layer of a self that was already being constructed through bodily experience. It teaches the I that it can become an object of its own attention.

## The strange loop
Once the I can look at itself, there is no going back.
We can begin to judge, admire, hide, or invent ourselves.
The mirror is the threshold where the "observer" and the "observed" meet.

If the mirror shows me who I am today, what connects me to the person I was yesterday?
To answer that, we must look at the thread that holds the I together across time.
We must look at memory.
