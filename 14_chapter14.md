# CHAPTER 14: THE BECOMING

We often spend much of our lives waiting to meet a person who has not yet arrived. 

We say: "When I finish studying, I will be ready." "When I get the job, I will be stable." "When I understand myself, I will finally know who I am." We imagine our identity as a destination, a final version of the self that is waiting somewhere on the horizon. 

But eventually, the I notices something strange: it is always on its way.

## The Illusion of the Finished Self
If the I is always changing, what does it mean to become? We have already dismantled the idea of a fixed, simple "I." We have seen it as something inherited, embodied, and influenced. Now we discover that there may be no final version waiting at the end.

## Dynamic Equilibrium: Stability through Change
In chemistry, a system can reach dynamic equilibrium. From the outside, it appears still and stable. But microscopically, the process is in constant motion—forward and reverse reactions continue at equal rates. 
The I is much like this. Stability is not the opposite of change; stability is a particular pattern of change. The body is not a statue. Cells are replaced, neural connections shift, and memories are updated. The living organism maintains continuity not by remaining the same, but through a continuous, maintained activity.

## The Feedback Loop of Being
I shape myself, and then the self I have shaped becomes part of what shapes me. This is the core of dynamic systems: past states and present actions create the constraints for future possibilities. The gardener from the previous chapter is not standing outside the garden; the gardener is one of the things growing.

## The Future I
Imagine meeting yourself ten years from now. That person has memories that do not yet exist and has survived losses you have not yet encountered. 
The future I is a person who does not yet exist, yet they already exert a causal power over the present. We sacrifice for them, we fear for them, and we make decisions today because of a person we have not yet met.

## Becoming is Neutral; Cultivation is Choice
Becoming is not necessarily improvement. Change can expand us or narrow us; it can heal or it can damage. While "becoming" is a biological and psychological inevitability, "cultivation" is the act of stewardship. 

## The Unfinished I
Perhaps the mystery is not how I remain the same, but how continuity is possible at all. Tomorrow, I will wake as someone who has never existed before, and yet I will call that person "me." 

I once thought becoming meant moving toward a destination. But perhaps becoming is not the road toward a final version of myself. Perhaps there is no final version.

I am not finished. I am becoming.
