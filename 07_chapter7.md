# CHAPTER 7: THE MEMORY OF I

There is a particular kind of strangeness in looking at a photograph of yourself as a child. You recognize the face immediately, but you cannot remember being that child. You cannot hear the thoughts behind those eyes.

The child is looking outward; the adult is looking backward. And between them lies something neither can fully recover: **the experience of becoming.**

## The stranger in the photograph
The photograph proves that the child existed. Memory is supposed to tell us that the child was us. But what happens when memory is missing?
Perhaps this is one of the strangest facts about being human: **I can know that I was someone I cannot remember being.**

## Memory as reconstruction
Memory is not a video archive. Every time I remember, I am remembering **now**.
The person remembering is not the person who experienced the event. The adult brings everything that happened afterward—knowledge, new beliefs, and new meanings—into the act of remembering.
I cannot change what happened, but I can change what what happened *means* to me.

## The memories others gave us
My mother or grandmother may remember something about me that I cannot. Eventually, their stories become part of my personal history. This creates a fascinating boundary: can I "remember" something I never personally experienced because the social mirror has provided the narrative?

## The Ship of Theseus
If a ship has every one of its planks replaced over time, is it still the same ship?
If the body changes, the beliefs change, the personality changes, and the memories change, what remains? What holds yesterday’s I to today’s I?
The I is not a permanent object travelling through time; it is a continuity being continuously produced.

## The end of Part II
We have moved through Part II: **Experience → Perception → Self-Recognition → Memory.**
We have not found a permanent, unchanging entity called the "I." Instead, we have found a process that experiences, interprets, and refers back to itself.
The I has become a strange thing: it perceives the world, it perceives itself, and it constructs a story about how it became itself.

But what happens when another "I" enters the picture?
When we realize we are not the only ones with a story?
We must move to the next investigation.
